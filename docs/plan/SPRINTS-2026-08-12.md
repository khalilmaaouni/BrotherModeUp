# Sprints and update cycle

Status: CURRENT

The cadence, then the ranked backlog for your approval at the Monday review.
Every item names which objective it serves. Anything that cannot name one is
in the parking lot at the bottom, never in the backlog.

---

## The cadence

| | |
|---|---|
| **Sprint length** | One week |
| **Boundary** | Monday morning. The review and the sprint turn happen the same day |
| **Review** | Sixty minutes, same agenda every week, chaired by a champion |
| **Change budget** | Exactly one process change per week |
| **Release** | Once per sprint, on the boundary, and only if fully green |
| **Backlog** | Proposed ranked, approved or reordered by you at the review |

**Why Monday rather than Friday.** A process change decided on Friday sits
untested over the weekend, and if it blocks somebody wrongly it does so on a
Monday with nobody around who chose it. Monday means the week's data is still
fresh from Friday and the change lands where people can react to it.

**Why exactly one change.** Change five things and a bad week teaches you
nothing. Change one and you know what caused the difference.

**Why a red sprint does not release.** The situation this prevents is the one
your machine is in right now: a published version newer than what anybody
actually runs. One predictable number per week that everybody installs is
worth more than shipping whenever something is ready.

**Sync with the sibling.** Both products review on the same Monday and
release on the same boundary, but they carry **independent version numbers**.
Forcing them to move together means one waits for the other with no benefit,
and tonight was a live example: this product was ready and the sibling was
not touched. The install card carries both numbers so a person still has one
thing to read.

---

## The two numbers that decide everything

Every review puts two numbers on the board, from the daily notes: **minutes
lost** against **things caught**.

- Minutes lost high, things caught near zero: the process costs more than it
  returns. Remove something.
- Things caught high: name what would have happened had it not been caught.
  That is the argument for keeping a gate when somebody complains.

Write both every week, including the embarrassing ones. A process that cannot
show its cost cannot defend its value.

## Parking lot

Items that cannot yet name which objective they serve. They stay here until
they can, which is the check that stops direction being lost.

- **A shared hosted store.** Named as expensive, not yet named as necessary.
  Would need sync rules, hosting, auth, and a migration for every existing
  record.
- **A seventh public command.** The surface consolidated inward on purpose in
  this release; adding one back needs a reason stronger than convenience.
- **Runtime ports** beyond what the runtimes table already marks verified.
  The table's own words say an unverified runtime should not be wired,
  because a guard that fails open while looking installed is worse than none.

## What would make me re-propose this ranking

If the first-day pack takes a champion materially longer than forty minutes,
or if either of them cannot explain the loop after three real changes, then
sprint three is too early and the ranking should slow down rather than push
on. Adding people does not fix a phase that is not working.
