# Daily note template

Status: CURRENT

Two minutes. Four lines. Every day you used the tools. Copy the block below,
date it, and write where your team keeps notes.

This is not a report and nobody is graded on it. It is the raw material the
weekly review reads out loud, and it is the only thing that stops the process
growing without limit.

---

```markdown
## YYYY-MM-DD

1. What I tried to do with the tools today:
   (one line, the task name is enough)

2. Where I got stuck, and for how long:
   (the command, the message, the minutes lost)

3. What the tool caught that I would have missed:
   (write "nothing" when it is nothing, that is data too)

4. One thing I would delete from the process:
   (always answer this one, even when it feels small)
```

---

## Rules for the note

**Four lines is enough.** If it is taking more than two minutes, you are
writing a report and the note will stop happening within a week.

**"Nothing" is a valid answer to question 3**, and a useful one. A week where
the tools caught nothing is an argument, and the review needs to hear it.

**Always answer question 4.** This is the question that keeps the process from
growing without limit. Every process anybody has ever adopted grew until
somebody was allowed to remove things, and this line is that permission,
written down.

**Minutes lost should be a real number**, even a rough one. "About 25 minutes"
is useful. "A while" is not, because the review adds these up and puts one
number on the board.

## What happens to your notes

At the weekly review they are read out loud, oldest first, with no discussion
during the reading. Then two numbers go on the board: minutes lost this week,
and things caught this week. Only items that appeared more than once get
discussed. Exactly one thing changes.

**A week of blank notes is a finding, not an absence of one.** It means the
process is too heavy, or people have stopped believing anybody reads them.
Both are worth knowing and both are fixable, but only if the blankness is
treated as data instead of as nothing having happened.
