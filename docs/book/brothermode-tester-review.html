Your first week with BrotherMode

You are one of the first people outside this project to install it. Your week is the first outside evidence this product has.

Time asked of you one real week, then twenty minutes Questions fifteen Collected automatically nothing

This is not a satisfaction survey. It is the only way one specific gap in this project gets closed, and the gap is named on its own roadmap.

BrotherMode's roadmap has six proof rungs, from planned up to certified. Rung five is verified externally: checked by somebody outside the project, on a machine the project does not own. That rung is empty. The roadmap prints it empty rather than hiding it, and nothing anyone writes in this repository can fill it.

You can. That is the whole reason you were sent this.

Before you start

Read the booklet first if you have not: it explains what this is, what it refuses to do, and when it is not worth the overhead. Twenty minutes, and it will make the week make sense.

Then install it by following README.md in the repository rather than any steps retyped here. Retyped steps drift; that page is held truthful by the project's own tests.

Two things to know before you hit a wall

On Windows the installer refuses to run natively. Install inside WSL instead; the README covers it. This is a known limit, not you doing it wrong.

If you use the /plugin install path, that path has been run very few times before yours, and by the author. Your run is a real test of it either way, and a rough one is useful information rather than a failure on your part.

Use it on something real

One real piece of work over the week. Not a project invented for the occasion, because an invented project will not put pressure on the parts that matter: crossing sessions, more than one thing running at once, a decision only you can make, a definition of done somebody would hold you to.

Report a break the moment it happens

Do not save it for the end of the week. The moment something breaks, write down what you were doing, what you expected, and what happened instead, and send it. A week-old memory of a bug is worth much less than a fresh one, and you will forget the detail that mattered.

What we do with your answers, and what we will not

This process collects nothing automatically. No telemetry is taken from your machine. Nothing reaches us unless you type it and send it yourself. We do not read your files, your project folder, or anything else on your machine to fill gaps in what you tell us.

We will use your answers to decide what to fix before anyone else installs this, and to write down honestly what a first week outside this project actually looked like. We will not share your answers outside this project, and we will not quote you anywhere public, without asking you first.

What would help most

A low score with a specific reason is worth more to us than a high score. If you abandoned it on day three, that is the single most valuable thing you can send, and question 14 is where to say so.

How to send it back

Two ways, your choice.

On GitHub. The repository is public, so you do not need to be given access. Three templates, pick the one that fits:

  • Week one feedback, for the fifteen questions above.
  • Install problem, if you never got it running.
  • Friction, for one small thing that annoyed you, when it is not worth a whole report.

Two things about that route, said plainly rather than discovered later: it needs a GitHub account, and what you file is public.

Or send it straight to whoever gave you this link. If any answer would be blunter in private, use this route. Question 14 is the one we actually need honest, and people soften in public. A private, unflattering answer is worth more to us than a polite public one.

There is no third option, and no form, email address or chat channel we have not named on this page.

A low score with a reason attached moves this project. A high score with nothing behind it does not.
