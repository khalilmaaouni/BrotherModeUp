The flow, and the loop nobody has built

The chain is usually drawn as a straight line from an idea to a release. It is not a line, it is a loop: what the change actually does in the world is where the next intent comes from. That return edge is drawn here in red because nothing carries it today, which is why a defect cannot be entered as a defect and a requirement discovered mid-build tells nobody.

exists and refuses something exists and can be walked past does not exist a person decides here

The colour on each stage is where that stage stands today, not where it is meant to end up. Four of the last five do not exist yet, and saying so is the point: a diagram that only draws the finished shape hides the work.

Every stage in detail

Human intent
Partial

A person wants something. The only stage no software may originate. Everything downstream exists either to carry it faithfully or to report honestly that it could not.

the goal, in the person’s own words what is in scope and what is out how we will know it worked the questions asked and the answers given, before planning starts

Where the gap is. The first three are recorded fields today. The fourth is not: nothing enforces or keeps a discussion before planning, so the exploration that produced the goal evaporates with the conversation. Two reviewers reported that independently, from different projects.

A requirement that appears during the build re-enters here, and today it tells nobody: a rule added after a test sheet was generated leaves that sheet one case short, silently.

Interchangeable does not mean optional. Most people arrive with none of these installed, and today that leaves this stage empty for them. BrotherMode owes a native floor: genuinely good on its own, walked through rather than written down, stepping aside the moment a real method is present, and never becoming a requirement. The routing that would defer to an installed method already runs. The floor it degrades to is a sentence, not a method.

↓
BrotherMode
One hole

Execution provenance: an account of what happened that somebody else can check. Not process compliance, not a diary. The record as it really occurred, including the parts that went badly. Six mechanisms produce it, and each produces a record rather than a feeling.

ground map before any write claims, one writer per file the work record and its lifecycle the done-check rule handover when a session ends escalation when stuck

Where the hole is, and it turned out to be worse than first drawn. The one-writer control is inert in two ways. In the assurance repository, roughly twenty legacy claim lines cannot be parsed and are not enforced. And on the machine that wrote this page, BrotherMode’s fence hook is not running at all: it appears zero times in every settings file, and this product is absent from the 68 registered plugins. A write across a live claim WAS refused during this session, and that refusal came from the sibling’s hook, which is registered. The protection was real and it was the other product’s.

The other five mechanisms ran. Everything invoked as a command works exactly as documented, because a command is not a hook. It is the automatic half, the part meant to fire without being remembered, that is inert on the install shape people actually use.

It does not decide whether a change is safe, classify risk, own review, or judge the work. A product that both does the work and grades it is grading itself.

↓
Change passport
Does not exist

The one object that travels from execution to assurance, and the only seam between the two products. Without it each side reaches into the other’s internals, both drift, and neither can ever be replaced. Five fields, and no more.

1. what was done: change identity, commit range, files 2. who did it: sessions, claims, the accountable person by name 3. what was run: each check, its result, and whether a build system or a laptop produced it 4. what was NOT established: mandatory, never empty 5. where it came from: the method, named and not judged

Field four is why the passport is worth carrying. A reviewer reads one line and knows where to spend attention. A tester who keeps finding interface problems nobody wrote down is handed the exact list of places nobody looked. A passport claiming nothing is unexamined is the precise lie this chain exists to prevent.

BrotherMode produces it, BrotherSBE consumes it, it never travels back. Anything assurance needs that it does not carry is a defect in the passport, not permission to reach into execution state.

↓
BrotherSBE
Five of eight

Assurance: it owns what must be proven, and verifies that it was. It borrows execution entirely, so a better coding agent or a better scanner makes it better. Eight concerns, each coloured by what exists today.

behaviour: the rules the software must follow under stated conditions business impact: what it is worth, and what getting it wrong costs risk: how bad failure would be, which decides how much ceremony required proof: what must be shown before this may proceed evidence integrity: whether the proof is real, current and traceable accountability: who decided, who reviewed, who owns the result release readiness: whether this may go, stated as a judgement production observation: what it did once it was actually running

Reading the colours. Green means it exists and refuses something: behaviour has a table whose check rejects four separate ways to fake it, and risk is computed from five intake answers. Amber means it exists and can be walked past: evidence binds to a commit but never records whether a build system or a laptop produced it, and reviewer load is invisible so one person silently becomes the queue. Red means nothing at all, and the three reds are the same three the whole chain is missing at the end.

↓
Human decision
Unconditional

A person decides to release, or not. This node may never be removed, shortened to a rubber stamp, or pre-approved. The system’s job is to make the decision cheap and well informed, never to make it unnecessary.

what was found the recommendation, and it is refused if absent the alternatives not tried the risk if the system guesses the one decision needed what happens if nobody answers, also refused if absent

Humans are in the loop at four points, not one: the intent that starts the chain, any forcing condition where guessing is the danger, this release decision, and the acceptance that closes it. A change that removes, bypasses or silently pre-answers any of the four is refused whatever it saves.

The packet above is built and refuses to print hollow. What is still missing is the other half: no record of an acceptance ever happening. So the system can now ask a person well, and still cannot say afterwards who said yes.

↓
Release
The host does this

The change reaches the world: a merge, a deploy, a published package, on GitHub or Bitbucket or whatever pipeline the team already runs. Both products stop at the pull request, so no piece of work may claim this stage as its own, and the queue check refuses one that tries.

the host merges, deploys or publishes the human decision must already be recorded: who accepted, when, against what that record does not exist today, so “who accepted this” has no answer

Why neither product performs it, and the second reason is the real one. Scope: a deployment platform is a different product and doing it badly is worse than not doing it. And structure: a system that can both approve and release has no gap in it for a person to stand in. Release is the last point where somebody can still say no, so the products stay on the near side of that line.

↓
Verified reality
Does not exist

Whether it actually worked, observed rather than asserted. The terminal state, and deliberately not “the gate went green”: a green gate is a claim about proof, verified reality is a claim about the world. Four things would count, none of them opinions.

no material reopen within seven days no rollback, incident or emergency fix against it the acceptance held: whoever accepted it did not come back the team’s own queue numbers moved, or did not

Why the two come apart constantly. Acceptance criteria describe core functions, while the problems a good tester finds are unnatural behaviour, misunderstandings from the design phase, and text a translator wrote without context. Every one of those sits comfortably inside a fully green change.

All four are red because nothing computes any of them. There is no post-merge record at all, so a risk tier can never be compared with its real outcome, and the agreed success measure cannot even be recomputed. The seven-day test already sat in the ratified delivery definition, which means the north star was reaching for this stage before the chain was ever drawn.

The same chain, walked four ways

One shape, and it should feel different depending on who you are. If it feels identical for all four, it is too heavy for the first and too light for the last.

One person, one small change

Intent is a sentence. The method is whatever they already do. Provenance costs seconds: a claim, a record, a done-check. The passport is thin and its fourth field is honest, because nothing was checked but the unit suite. Assurance sits at the lowest tier, two artifacts rather than seven. The decision-maker is the same person, and the system says so plainly instead of pretending a review happened. This path has to stay fast, because a skipped path records nothing at all.

A team, a risky migration

Intent is a specification. Assurance is the highest tier: both directions of the migration proven against a restore, real row counts, an approval signed by somebody other than the author, and evidence produced by the build system rather than a laptop. The passport’s fourth field is long, and it is the most valuable page in the change. The decision belongs to a named accountable person, and the release waits for them.

Somebody who does not write code

An analyst or a tester meets the chain at three points only: stating the intent, writing what the software must do under stated conditions, and deciding whether what came back is right. They should never meet a claim, a fence, a risk tier or a receipt. The measure of whether this works for them is whether their first hour goes on the work or on installing prerequisites, and today it goes on prerequisites.

A consultancy proving diligence

Here the chain is the deliverable. Every stage leaves a record a client can read: what was intended, what was decided and why, what was proven, what was honestly not proven, who accepted it, and what happened afterwards. The fourth passport field is what makes it credible rather than marketing, because a report listing only what went well gets read as one.

The two rules this imposes on everything

Rule one: the human loop is never optimised away

Humans stay in the loop at four points: the intent that starts the chain, any forcing condition where guessing is the danger, the release decision, and the acceptance that closes it. A change that removes, bypasses or silently pre-answers one of those is refused, whatever it saves.

This is a mechanism, not an intention. A stuck run now converts itself into a named decision for a person, with a recommendation and a default action, on four triggers: a danger where guessing is the risk, three distinct approaches failed, two failures sharing one root cause, or a spent budget. It never answers the question itself and never decides on anyone’s behalf.

Rule two: every item of work names its stage

Work that cannot name the stage it serves goes to the parking lot, not the backlog. This is now checked rather than remembered: the queue knows the fourteen stage names, an item naming one that does not exist fails hard and names the item, and an item naming none is reported on every run without breaking anything. Today 37 queued items name no stage, and the check says so out loud.

Where the gaps came from, which is the part worth trusting

Nine holes were found earlier the same day by walking the lifecycle and asking what has no tool at all, before this chain was written down. Five of the nine turned out to be one hole seen five times: the system is complete up to the merge and blind after it.

This chain names that same hole from the opposite direction. Its last four stages are exactly the four that do not exist. The gaps are not scattered debt, they are the unbuilt half of the model, and two independent passes reached it within an hour of each other.
