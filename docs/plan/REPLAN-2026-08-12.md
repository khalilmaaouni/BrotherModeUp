Status: CURRENT

## 1. Why the first plan scored 3 out of 10

The founder's words: the forecasting was poor, and **the plan would have
stopped work during the night if he had gone to bed.**

That is two failures and the second is the serious one.

### Failure A: the estimates were wrong in one direction, by a lot

| Task | Forecast | Actual | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tag cut and pushed | by 02:00 | 00:27 | ~4x fast |
| Install rehearsed | by 04:00 | 00:45 | ~5x fast |
| Full battery | 10 to 20 min | 12.5 min | accurate |
| Whole release path | overnight, 8 hours | ~90 minutes | ~5x fast |

The only accurate estimate was the one measured from a previous run of the
same command. **Every estimate derived from judgement was wrong by four to
five times, all in the same direction.**

Root cause: the estimates were sized in "how long would a careful person take
on this" units. That is the wrong unit. The work was done by an agent that
does not type, does not context-switch, and does not tire, but which does pay
a real cost for every wrong turn. Wrong turns, not typing, are the actual
cost driver, and they are bounded by how well-specified the task is, not by
its size.

### Failure B, the serious one: the night had no depth

The plan contained thirteen tasks. Thirteen tasks is a plan for the work, not
a plan for the **night**. When they finished in ninety minutes there was
nothing queued behind them, and the only thing still armed was a scheduled
task restricted to board refreshes and mechanical steps.

Had the founder gone to bed at midnight as he intended, roughly six of the
eight hours would have produced nothing.

**A plan whose completion leaves the machine idle is not finished being
written.** An overnight plan is not a list of what must happen. It is a queue
deep enough that finishing early is impossible.

## 2. The forecasting rule, changed

Binding from now on.

1. **Estimate from measured throughput, not from imagined effort.** If a
   comparable task has been run before, quote its actual duration. If it has
   not, say so and give a range whose lower bound is a quarter of the
   instinctive guess.
2. **State the estimate as a range with its assumption**, never a point, and
   name what would make it the slow end. Tonight the honest form was: "ninety
   minutes if the battery passes first time, four hours if it needs two runs
   and a fix in between."
3. **Every overnight plan carries at least 3x more queued work than its
   forecast needs.** Depth is the deliverable. Running out of queue is a
   planning defect, reported as one.
4. **The queue is strictly ordered and every item is independently startable.**
   No item may block on a founder answer, because he is asleep. Anything that
   does goes to the founder-gated list and never into the queue.
5. **Record actual against forecast at every closed loop.** Two data points
   already exist tonight and they are in the table above. Without this the
   same error repeats forever.

release per boundary and only if green, both products reviewing together and
versioning independently, backlog proposed ranked and approved by the founder.

The one change this replan makes to it: **every sprint plan now carries its
own overflow queue**, for the same reason the night did. A sprint that
finishes its committed work on Thursday should not spend Friday idle, and the
overflow is chosen in advance so that choice is not made while tired.
